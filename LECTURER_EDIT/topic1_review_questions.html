Topic 1 Review Questions

Use these questions after studying the notes. They are for revision and discussion rather than formal grading. Suggested answers are provided for self-checking.

A. Core understanding

1. In your own words, define Data Science.
Suggested answer
Data Science is an interdisciplinary field that combines data, computing, statistical methods and domain knowledge to generate insights, answer questions and support decisions.
2. What is Artificial Intelligence, and how is it different from Data Science?
Suggested answer
AI focuses on systems that perform tasks associated with intelligent behaviour, such as learning, reasoning or recognition. Data Science focuses more broadly on using data to understand problems and support decisions. The fields overlap, but neither is identical to the other.
3. Explain the relationship between AI and Machine Learning.
Suggested answer
Machine Learning is one major approach within AI. It allows models to learn patterns from data rather than requiring every rule to be written explicitly.
4. Why is domain knowledge important in a Data Science project?
Suggested answer
Domain knowledge helps define the right problem, select meaningful variables, judge assumptions, interpret findings correctly and decide whether the output makes practical sense.
5. List the main stages of the simplified Data Science workflow presented in the notes.
Suggested answer
Define the problem; collect data; clean and prepare; explore and analyse; model when needed; evaluate; communicate and act.
6. What is a model, and why should a model output not automatically be treated as truth?
Suggested answer
A model is a simplified representation of a relationship or process. Because it simplifies reality and is built from imperfect data and assumptions, it can make errors or fail in new settings.

B. History and evolution

7. What three major forces have strongly accelerated modern Data Science and AI?
Suggested answer
Increasing availability of digital data, greater computing power and improved algorithms. Wider connectivity and storage have also contributed.
8. What was the main idea behind expert systems?
Suggested answer
Expert systems attempted to encode specialist knowledge using explicit rules so that computers could make decisions or recommendations in narrow domains.
9. How did the shift toward statistical and machine-learning methods change AI development?
Suggested answer
Instead of relying only on manually written rules, systems increasingly learned patterns from data, making it possible to address complex recognition and prediction tasks.
10. What is generative AI? Give two types of content it can generate.
Suggested answer
Generative AI refers to models that can create new content based on learned patterns. Examples include text, images, audio, video and software code.

C. Application and critical thinking

11. A university wants to calculate every student's average mark. Does this task require AI? Explain.
Suggested answer
No. A straightforward arithmetic algorithm is sufficient. This illustrates that not every computational or data task needs AI.
12. Give one example of Data Science or AI in agriculture and identify the data it would require.
Suggested answer
Example: crop-disease recognition may require labelled crop images. Yield forecasting may require historical yields, weather, soil, management and location data.
13. Suppose a bank develops a credit-risk model using historical loan data that underrepresent rural farmers. What problem might this cause?
Suggested answer
The model may generalise poorly to rural farmers or produce unfair outcomes because the training data do not adequately represent that group.
14. Why should human judgement remain important when AI is used in health, finance or education?
Suggested answer
AI outputs can be wrong, biased or incomplete. Human experts provide context, accountability, ethical judgement and the ability to consider information not represented in the model.
15. Identify a problem in Tanzania that could benefit from Data Science or AI. Describe: (a) the problem, (b) data needed, (c) the likely analysis or AI task, and (d) one risk.
Suggested answer
Answers will vary. A strong response should clearly connect the problem to relevant data, an appropriate analysis or model, and a realistic limitation such as data quality, privacy, bias, infrastructure or incorrect predictions.
