Topic 2 Review Questions

Use these questions for revision, tutorial discussion and self-study. Suggested answers can be revealed after attempting each question.

A. Data sources and types

1. Distinguish primary data from secondary data.
Suggested answer
Primary data are collected specifically for the current purpose; secondary data already exist because they were collected previously for another purpose.
2. Give two examples each of structured and unstructured data.
Suggested answer
Structured: database table, spreadsheet. Unstructured: image, audio, video or free text.
3. Why is a telephone number not normally treated as a numerical measurement?
Suggested answer
Although it contains digits, it identifies a telephone endpoint; arithmetic operations such as averaging telephone numbers have no meaningful interpretation.
4. What is metadata and why is it useful?
Suggested answer
Metadata describe the dataset, such as field definitions, units, allowed values and data types. They help users interpret and reuse data correctly.
5. A researcher downloads rainfall data from a national meteorological database. Is this primary or secondary data for the researcher? Explain.
Suggested answer
It is secondary data because the researcher is reusing data previously collected by another organisation.

B. Data cleaning

6. List five common data-quality problems.
Suggested answer
Examples: missing values, duplicates, inconsistent formats, invalid values, data-entry errors, outliers and wrong data types.
7. Why should an outlier not automatically be deleted?
Suggested answer
It may be a genuine extreme observation rather than an error. The analyst should investigate the context and source before deciding what to do.
8. Why can filling all missing values with the mean be problematic?
Suggested answer
It can change the distribution, reduce natural variation and introduce misleading values. The treatment should depend on why data are missing and how the field is used.
9. Why is a repeated name alone insufficient evidence that two rows are duplicates?
Suggested answer
Different people can have the same name. Stronger identifiers such as a unique student ID or a combination of fields are needed.
10. Explain the cleaning cycle: inspect, detect, correct, validate.
Suggested answer
Inspect the dataset and its structure; detect problems; make justified corrections or flags; then validate that the corrections are sensible and did not introduce new errors.
11. Why should the original raw dataset be preserved?
Suggested answer
It provides an unchanged reference, makes the process reproducible and allows mistakes in cleaning to be traced or reversed.

C. Preprocessing and application

12. Explain the difference between data cleaning and data preprocessing.
Suggested answer
Cleaning focuses on identifying and correcting quality problems. Preprocessing is broader and includes cleaning plus transformations such as standardising formats, encoding categories, scaling and selecting data.
13. Give an example of a format that may need standardisation.
Suggested answer
Dates may appear in different formats, categories may differ by capitalisation, or measurements may use different units.
14. Why might categorical data need encoding?
Suggested answer
Many computational and ML methods require numerical inputs, so categories may need an appropriate numerical representation without changing their meaning.
15. Why can numerical scaling be useful?
Suggested answer
Some algorithms are sensitive to variables with very different numerical ranges. Scaling makes their representations more comparable.
16. Give two examples of derived variables.
Suggested answer
Age derived from date of birth; month or day of week derived from a timestamp; monthly expenditure derived by aggregating transactions.
17. In the provided messy student dataset, identify at least five problems you would investigate.
Suggested answer
Examples include a missing age, duplicate DS005, inconsistent region spellings/capitalisation, age recorded as “twenty”, score 103, attendance 105, missing attendance, “-” region, whitespace around 67 and N/A score.
18. A dataset has 10,000 rows but was collected only from urban users. Can it automatically be described as high quality for studying the whole national population?
Suggested answer
No. Large size does not guarantee representativeness. The sampling process and intended population must be considered.
