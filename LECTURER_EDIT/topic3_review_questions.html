Topic 3 Review Questions

Use these questions for tutorial discussion, self-study and revision. Attempt each question before opening the suggested answer.

A. EDA and descriptive statistics

1. What is Exploratory Data Analysis?
Suggested answer
EDA is the process of examining a dataset using summaries, visualisations and questions to understand its structure, patterns, variability and unusual observations before deeper analysis.
2. Why is EDA described as iterative?
Suggested answer
Exploration often reveals new questions or data-quality problems, so the analyst may return to cleaning, create new summaries and visualise the data again.
3. Distinguish mean, median and mode.
Suggested answer
Mean is the arithmetic average; median is the middle ordered value; mode is the most frequently occurring value or category.
4. When may the median be more informative than the mean?
Suggested answer
When a numerical distribution is skewed or contains extreme values that strongly influence the mean.
5. Why do measures of spread matter?
Suggested answer
Two datasets may have the same centre but very different variability. Spread shows how dispersed or consistent the observations are.
6. Name three measures of spread.
Suggested answer
Range, interquartile range, variance and standard deviation are examples.

B. Visualisation

7. What is the main difference between a bar chart and a histogram?
Suggested answer
A bar chart compares categories, while a histogram shows the distribution of a numerical variable across intervals.
8. Which chart would you use to examine monthly rainfall over three years? Why?
Suggested answer
A line chart is appropriate because the observations are ordered over time and the aim is to see change and trend.
9. Which chart is useful for examining the relationship between study hours and final score?
Suggested answer
A scatter plot because both variables are numerical and each point can represent one student's pair of values.
10. Give three features of a well-designed chart.
Suggested answer
Examples: clear title, labelled axes, correct units, suitable scale, appropriate chart type and absence of distracting decoration.
11. How can a misleading axis influence interpretation?
Suggested answer
A truncated or inappropriate scale can visually exaggerate or minimise differences and make a pattern appear stronger or weaker than it really is.

C. Patterns and interpretation

12. What kinds of patterns are commonly examined during EDA?
Suggested answer
Distributions, relationships, group differences, trends and anomalies.
13. What does a positive relationship between two numerical variables mean?
Suggested answer
In general, larger values of one variable tend to occur with larger values of the other, although the relationship may be weak or affected by other factors.
14. Why does correlation not automatically prove causation?
Suggested answer
Other variables may influence both variables, the causal direction may be different from what is assumed, or the association may be coincidental.
15. What is an anomaly and how should it be handled during EDA?
Suggested answer
An anomaly is an observation that differs from the general pattern. It should be investigated and verified before deciding whether it is an error or a meaningful extreme case.
16. A class has an average score of 70. What important information is missing from this statement?
Suggested answer
We do not know the distribution, spread, number of students, possible outliers or whether different groups have different score patterns.
17. If two regions have average scores of 75 and 68, what else would you check before concluding one region performs better?
Suggested answer
Group sizes, score distributions, variability, data quality, sampling differences and other factors that may differ between the regions.
18. Using the Topic 3 dataset, formulate one descriptive question, one comparison question and one relationship question.
Suggested answer
Examples: What is the typical final score? Do average scores differ by region? Is attendance associated with final score?
19. What turns an observed pattern into an insight?
Suggested answer
An insight connects the observed pattern to context, explains why it matters and identifies a useful implication or a question for further investigation.
20. Why should EDA observations be written carefully as evidence rather than conclusions that go beyond the data?
Suggested answer
EDA can reveal patterns and associations, but without appropriate study design or further analysis it may not justify causal or generalised claims.
