Topic 4 Review Questions

Use these questions for revision, tutorial discussion and self-study. Suggested answers can be revealed after you attempt each question.

A. Probability and distributions

1. What does probability measure?
Suggested answer
Probability measures how likely an event is, using values between 0 and 1.
2. What is an event in probability?
Suggested answer
An event is an outcome or condition of interest, such as “the student passes” or “it rains today”.
3. What is a random variable?
Suggested answer
A random variable is a numerical representation of possible outcomes.
4. Distinguish a discrete distribution from a continuous distribution.
Suggested answer
A discrete distribution describes separated outcomes such as counts, while a continuous distribution describes measured values that vary on a continuum.
5. What is the expected value in intuitive terms?
Suggested answer
It is a long-run average or central value suggested by the probability model.
6. Name one common discrete distribution and one common continuous distribution.
Suggested answer
Discrete: binomial. Continuous: normal distribution.

B. Hypothesis testing

7. What is the purpose of hypothesis testing?
Suggested answer
It helps evaluate a claim using sample evidence and determine whether the evidence is strong enough to challenge a default assumption.
8. What does the null hypothesis usually represent?
Suggested answer
A default claim such as no difference, no effect or no relationship.
9. What does the alternative hypothesis represent?
Suggested answer
The competing claim, such as a difference or an effect being present.
10. In simple terms, what does a p-value describe?
Suggested answer
It describes how unusual the observed result would be if the null hypothesis were true.
11. Why is “fail to reject the null hypothesis” different from “prove the null hypothesis true”?
Suggested answer
Because failing to reject simply means the sample evidence was not strong enough against the null; it does not establish that the null is certainly true.
12. Why does statistical significance not automatically mean practical importance?
Suggested answer
A result can be statistically detectable yet still too small to matter in practice.

C. Regression analysis and interpretation

13. What is the main purpose of regression analysis?
Suggested answer
To model, describe or predict how an outcome variable changes in relation to one or more predictor variables.
14. In simple linear regression, what does the slope describe?
Suggested answer
It describes how much the outcome tends to change when the predictor increases by one unit.
15. What is a residual?
Suggested answer
A residual is the difference between an observed outcome and the value predicted by the regression model.
16. Why should predictions beyond the observed data range be treated carefully?
Suggested answer
Because the fitted relationship may not continue in the same way outside the range of data used to build the model.
17. A regression line slopes upward. What does that suggest?
Suggested answer
It suggests a positive association: as the predictor increases, the outcome tends to increase.
18. Why does an upward regression line not prove causation?
Suggested answer
Other variables or explanations may exist, and the observed relationship alone is not enough to prove a causal mechanism.
19. Give one real-world example where regression could be useful.
Suggested answer
Examples include predicting crop yield from rainfall and fertiliser use, estimating sales from advertising expenditure, or relating study hours to examination scores.
20. What is the most important attitude when interpreting Topic 4 results?
Suggested answer
Use the statistical result as evidence, but interpret it carefully and in context without overclaiming.
