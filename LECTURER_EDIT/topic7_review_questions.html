Topic 7 Review Questions

Use these questions for revision, tutorial discussion and self-study. Attempt each question before opening the suggested answer.

A. Ethical issues and privacy

1. Why should ethics be considered throughout the Data Science and AI lifecycle?
Suggested answer
Because choices made during problem definition, data collection, modelling, evaluation and deployment can all create benefits or harms.
2. What is data minimisation?
Suggested answer
Collecting only the personal information that is reasonably necessary for the intended purpose.
3. What is purpose limitation?
Suggested answer
Using personal data for a clear, legitimate and stated purpose rather than reusing it freely for unrelated purposes.
4. Distinguish privacy from data security.
Suggested answer
Privacy asks whether personal information is collected and used appropriately; security focuses on protecting information against unauthorised access, loss or misuse.
5. Why may removing names be insufficient to anonymise a dataset?
Suggested answer
Other fields or combinations of fields may still allow individuals to be identified.

B. Bias and fairness

6. What is historical bias?
Suggested answer
Bias arising because historical records reflect existing inequalities or past decisions.
7. What is sampling bias?
Suggested answer
Bias caused when the collected sample does not adequately represent the population or users for whom the system is intended.
8. Give an example of measurement bias.
Suggested answer
A sensor or proxy variable that measures the intended characteristic more accurately for one group than another.
9. What is label bias?
Suggested answer
Bias arising when training labels reflect inconsistent, subjective or systematically distorted human judgements.
10. Why can overall accuracy hide a fairness problem?
Suggested answer
Different groups may experience different error rates even when the combined overall accuracy appears good.
11. Why is fairness context dependent?
Suggested answer
Different error types and outcomes matter differently in different applications, so the relevant fairness goal depends on the decision and potential harm.

C. Transparency, accountability and society

12. What is transparency in an AI system?
Suggested answer
Providing appropriate information about how a system is developed, used and limited.
13. What is explainability?
Suggested answer
The ability to provide understandable reasons or factors that help people make sense of a system's output.
14. What does accountability mean in responsible AI?
Suggested answer
People or organisations remain responsible for system design, deployment, monitoring and corrective action.
15. Why can human oversight be important?
Suggested answer
It can enable review of unusual cases, correction of errors, consideration of context and a route for appeal in important decisions.
16. Name three potential societal benefits of AI.
Suggested answer
Examples include better decision support, improved accessibility, faster services, scientific discovery and automation of repetitive work.
17. Name three potential societal risks of AI.
Suggested answer
Examples include privacy loss, unfair discrimination, misinformation, over-reliance, unequal access, employment disruption and environmental cost.

D. Responsible AI practice

18. Why is responsible AI more than adding a disclaimer?
Suggested answer
Responsible AI requires practical safeguards such as data review, fairness checks, transparency, oversight, monitoring and accountability.
19. What should be monitored after deployment?
Suggested answer
Performance, subgroup errors, changing user behaviour, emerging harms, misuse and changes in the deployment environment.
20. A model is highly accurate but uses unnecessary sensitive personal data and offers no appeal process. Is it responsible? Explain.
Suggested answer
Not necessarily. Technical accuracy does not address privacy, proportionality, accountability or human oversight. Responsible AI requires more than predictive performance.
