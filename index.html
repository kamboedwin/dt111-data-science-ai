DT 111: Introduction to Data Science and AI

A beginner-friendly, offline interactive learning package for understanding the foundations, workflow, applications and responsible use of Data Science and Artificial Intelligence.

Course aim

Introduce the fundamental concepts, techniques and tools of Data Science and Artificial Intelligence, providing a solid foundation for further study and application.

Course structure

Core course · 12 credits · 120 hours

The approved curriculum contains eight major topics, combining concepts, practical work, applications and responsible AI.

Topic 1 summary

  • Data Science combines data, computing, statistics and domain knowledge to generate insight and support decisions.
  • Artificial Intelligence focuses on systems that perform tasks associated with intelligent behaviour.
  • Machine Learning is a major AI approach in which systems learn patterns from data.
  • A typical Data Science workflow moves from a problem through data collection, preparation, analysis, modelling, evaluation and communication.
  • The evolution of Data Science and AI has been accelerated by increasing data availability, computing power and algorithmic advances.
  • Applications span agriculture, health, finance, education, transport, environment and many other sectors.
  • Benefits must be considered together with limitations, privacy, fairness, data quality and the need for human judgement.

Topic 2 summary

  • Data may be primary or secondary, internal or external, and structured, semi-structured or unstructured.
  • Variable meaning matters: identifiers are different from quantities even when both contain digits.
  • Data quality includes completeness, accuracy, consistency, validity and uniqueness.
  • Cleaning addresses problems such as missing values, duplicates, errors and inconsistent formats.
  • Outliers should be investigated rather than automatically removed.
  • Preprocessing is broader than cleaning and can include encoding, scaling, transformation and selection.
  • Raw data should be preserved and cleaning decisions documented.

Topic 3 summary

  • EDA is an iterative process of understanding, summarising, visualising and questioning data.
  • Measures of centre describe typical values; measures of spread describe variability.
  • Bar charts compare categories, histograms show numerical distributions, line charts show ordered change and scatter plots examine relationships.
  • EDA looks for distributions, relationships, group differences, trends and anomalies.
  • Charts should be selected to answer specific questions and designed honestly.
  • Association does not automatically establish causation.
  • An insight combines an observed pattern with context, significance and a useful next question or action.

Topic 4 summary

  • Probability provides a way to reason about uncertainty.
  • Distributions describe how values or probabilities are spread.
  • Hypothesis testing evaluates a claim by comparing evidence to a null hypothesis.
  • A p-value expresses how unusual the data would be if the null hypothesis were true.
  • Regression analysis describes or predicts how an outcome changes with a predictor.
  • Statistical significance is not the same as practical importance.
  • Association patterns should be interpreted carefully and do not automatically imply causation.

Topic 5 summary

  • Supervised learning uses known targets; unsupervised learning searches for structure without target labels.
  • Regression predicts numbers, classification predicts classes and clustering discovers groups.
  • Linear regression can be used as a supervised learning algorithm for numerical prediction.
  • Decision trees make predictions by following a sequence of simple feature-based questions.
  • K-means groups similar observations into K clusters.
  • Models should be evaluated on unseen data to assess generalisation.
  • Overfitting and underfitting are two ways a model can fail to generalise well.

Topic 6 summary

  • AI is broader than Machine Learning and includes search, reasoning, planning, perception and language technologies.
  • Search problems can be represented using states, actions and goals.
  • BFS explores level by level, DFS follows branches deeply, and A* uses heuristic guidance.
  • Logic-based AI represents facts and rules explicitly and uses inference to derive conclusions.
  • Neural networks use weighted connections, hidden layers and training to learn complex patterns.
  • Different AI techniques are suitable for different kinds of problems.

Topic 7 summary

  • Ethical questions arise across the full Data Science and AI lifecycle.
  • Privacy concerns appropriate collection and use of personal data; security concerns protecting those data.
  • Bias can arise from history, sampling, measurement, labels and deployment.
  • Fairness requires examining who experiences which benefits, harms and error types.
  • Transparency, explainability, accountability and human oversight support responsible use.
  • AI can create major social benefits while also introducing risks such as exclusion, misinformation, surveillance and unequal access.
  • Responsible AI requires practical safeguards, monitoring and human responsibility.

Topic 8 summary

  • AI-powered assistive technologies can support communication, vision, physical access and cognitive participation.
  • Personalised learning can adapt content, pace and interaction, but should preserve learner choice and teacher judgement.
  • Accessible mobility systems need accurate information about ramps, lifts, surfaces, entrances and transport conditions.
  • AI can provide low-risk emotional well-being support, but should not replace qualified clinical care or emergency response.
  • Inclusive AI should be designed with intended users, provide alternatives, protect sensitive data and communicate limitations.
  • The value of contemporary AI should be judged by meaningful access, autonomy and participation—not novelty alone.

Assignment 8

Inclusive AI Solution Concept

Assistive technology, personalised learning, accessibility or well-being support

Purpose

Design a simple AI-supported concept that addresses one of the four curriculum areas in Topic 8 while demonstrating inclusive and responsible design.

Download Topic 8 scenarios

Task

  1. Choose one focus area: assistive communication/physical support, personalised learning, mobility/accessibility, or emotional well-being support.
  2. Define a specific user need or barrier. Avoid beginning with the technology; begin with the problem.
  3. Describe the proposed AI-supported solution and the information it would need.
  4. Explain how the user would interact with the system, including at least one alternative interaction mode.
  5. Identify at least three risks or failure cases and explain how each would be reduced.
  6. Explain how users with relevant lived experience would participate in requirements gathering or testing.
  7. State where human oversight, user control or escalation is required.
  8. Sketch a simple flow diagram showing: User need → Inputs → AI function → Accessible output → User feedback/human support.

Expected submission

A concise 3–5 page concept note including the problem, proposed solution, inclusion safeguards and one diagram.

For emotional well-being concepts: keep the proposed system within low-risk supportive functions. It should not autonomously diagnose disorders, prescribe treatment or replace emergency/professional care.
